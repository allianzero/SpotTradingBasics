# SpotTradingBasics

Spot trading basics: orders, charts, risk, and common beginner mistakes. "Spot" means you buy and sell the actual asset, right now, at the current price — not a leveraged bet on its future price.

> This guide is educational only, not financial advice. Trading is risky, and most short-term traders lose money. Never trade with money you cannot afford to lose.

## 1. What spot trading is

When you buy crypto on the spot market, you own the asset immediately. If you buy 0.1 BTC, you own 0.1 BTC, and you can withdraw it to your own wallet.

This is different from:

- **Futures and margin trading:** you borrow money or bet on price direction with leverage. Losses can exceed your deposit, and positions can be liquidated.
- **Options:** contracts that give you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell later.

Spot trading has no liquidation and no borrowed money. You can still lose money if the price falls — but you cannot lose more than you put in, and nobody can force-sell your position.

## 2. The order book, in plain language

Every exchange has an **order book**: a list of buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks) for a trading pair, such as BTC/USDT.

- The **bid** is the highest price someone is willing to pay right now.
- The **ask** is the lowest price someone is willing to sell for right now.
- The **spread** is the gap between them. A small spread means a liquid, easy-to-trade market. A large spread means trading will be expensive.

When you place an order, you either join the book (waiting for someone to match you) or take from the book (buying or selling immediately at the best available price).

## 3. Order types you will actually use

### Market order
Buy or sell immediately at the best available price.

- Pros: instant, simple.
- Cons: in a fast-moving or thin market, the price you get can be worse than the price you saw. This is called **slippage**.

### Limit order
Buy or sell only at a price you set, or better.

- Example: "Buy BTC only if it falls to $60,000 or below."
- Pros: you control the price.
- Cons: the order may never fill if the price never reaches your level.

### Stop-limit order
An order that activates when the price hits a trigger, then becomes a limit order.

- Common use: a stop-loss, to sell automatically if the price falls to a level you chose, limiting your loss.
- Warning: in a fast crash, the price can jump past your limit and the order may not fill. It reduces risk, it does not remove it.

**Beginner advice:** start with market and limit orders only. Learn stop-limit orders on paper (or with tiny amounts) before relying on them.

## 4. Reading a chart without overcomplicating it

A price chart shows price over time. The most common view is the **candlestick chart**: each candle shows the open, high, low, and close for a period.

- A green candle closed higher than it opened. A red candle closed lower.
- A long candle means a big move in that period. A short candle means a small move.
- **Volume** (bars at the bottom) shows how much was traded. Big moves on high volume are more meaningful than big moves on tiny volume.

Two ideas beginners overuse:

- **Support:** a price level where buying has repeatedly appeared. It can hold, or it can break.
- **Resistance:** a price level where selling has repeatedly appeared. Same warning.

Neither is a wall. Levels break all the time, especially in crypto. Treat them as zones of interest, not guarantees.

## 5. Fees: the cost you forget

Trading costs money even when you are "right":

- **Trading fee:** a percentage of each trade, charged by the exchange.
- **Spread:** the gap between bid and ask, which you pay when you buy at the ask and sell at the bid.
- **Withdrawal fee:** charged when you move crypto off the exchange.
- **Network fee:** charged by the blockchain when you move crypto on-chain.

Frequent trading multiplies fees. A strategy that looks profitable before fees can be unprofitable after them. Check the fee schedule of your exchange before you trade, and count fees in every calculation.

## 6. Risk management: the part that matters most

Most beginners focus on picking the right coin. Professionals focus on surviving being wrong. Use these rules:

1. **Position size:** never put a large share of your funds into a single trade. A common beginner rule is to risk only a small percentage of your total funds on any one idea.
2. **Know your exit before you enter.** Decide in advance: at what price will you sell for a profit, and at what price will you sell to cut a loss?
3. **Use stop-losses, but understand them.** They limit losses in normal markets, but they can fail in fast crashes and they can be triggered by normal volatility.
4. **Do not chase pumps.** Buying something because it is already rising fast usually means buying from people who bought earlier and cheaper.
5. **Do not average down blindly.** Buying more of a falling asset lowers your average price, but it also increases your total loss if it keeps falling. Have a reason, not just hope.
6. **Keep a trading journal.** Write down why you entered, what your plan was, and what happened. Reviewing it teaches more than any indicator.
7. **Take profits deliberately.** A profit you never take is not a profit. Decide your selling plan in advance, not in the excitement of a rise.

## 7. Psychology: why trading is hard

- **FOMO (fear of missing out):** buying because everyone else is, usually near the top.
- **Loss aversion:** holding a losing trade too long because selling feels like admitting a mistake, while selling winners too early to "lock in" a gain.
- **Revenge trading:** trading bigger after a loss to "win it back", which usually creates a bigger loss.
- **Overconfidence after wins:** a few good trades make you feel skilled, so you take bigger risks — just before the market reminds you otherwise.

If you notice yourself trading because of emotion rather than a plan, stop for the day. The market will still be there tomorrow.

## 8. Common beginner mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Trading with leverage on day one | Liquidation can wipe you out in minutes | Learn spot first, for months |
| Putting everything in one coin | One bad project can take everything | Diversify, and size positions |
| Buying on hype from social media | You are usually the exit liquidity | Research before you buy |
| Ignoring fees | Fees quietly eat small gains | Count fees in every trade |
| No exit plan | You hold losers and sell winners early | Plan entry, profit target, and stop before trading |
| Checking prices constantly | Leads to emotional, impulsive trades | Check on a schedule, not every minute |
| Trading money you need | Fear makes every decision worse | Only trade money you can afford to lose |

## 9. A simple first-month plan

1. Week 1: learn the exchange interface with tiny amounts. Place a market order, a limit order, and cancel an unfilled order.
2. Week 2: practise reading charts and writing down a plan before each trade — entry, target, stop, and reason.
3. Week 3: trade only your plan, with small sizes, and keep a journal of every trade.
4. Week 4: review the journal. What worked? What was emotion, not plan? What did fees cost you?

If after a month you are not consistently following your own plan, you are not ready for larger sizes — and that is a useful, cheap lesson.

## 10. Frequently asked questions

**Should I day trade or hold long term?**
Day trading requires time, skill, and emotional control, and most people lose money at it. Holding long term is simpler and avoids most fees and emotional trades, but still carries full price risk. Neither is safe.

**What is a good win rate?**
Win rate alone means little. A trader who wins 40% of trades but wins big and loses small can profit, while a trader who wins 80% but loses big on the rest can lose. Risk per trade matters more than win rate.

**Do indicators predict the future?**
No. Indicators summarise the past. They can help you describe a trend, but they do not guarantee what happens next, especially in volatile crypto markets.

**Can I copy someone else's trades?**
Copying removes your understanding of why a trade was made, so you will not know when to exit. If you follow someone, understand their plan — or do not follow them.

**Is spot trading "safe"?**
No. Spot trading removes leverage risk, but price risk remains fully. Crypto prices can fall 50% or more and stay down for a long time.
